1.6.5 is the iOS app's release, and the web board's. On iOS the board now
groups sessions by agent, with a column for each agent on an iPad, and the app
has had an accessibility pass: a stale card keeps its words, a card fits a
phone at the largest text sizes, VoiceOver hears a card's state second and
hears a session become blocked, Increase Contrast is honoured, and the usage
chart tells its lines apart by pattern and shape as well as colour. It goes to
the App Store as 1.6.5 (build 7), the first update since 1.5.0, so it also
brings 1.6.2's safer pairing to iPhones and iPads. The web board takes the
same chart, the same contrast values and the same stale cards, and gains a
table of each day's figures; the chart's patterns and the contrast values have
been live on agstatus.online since November 6. Android's stale cards and usage
chart match as well, in an app that is still unpublished. Nothing in the CLI, the hook or the
plugin changed, nor in the server's code; their version moves so that all of
them agree, and the server image carries the new web board.

## The iOS board, sorted by agent

A board with Claude and Codex sessions in one list made the reader sort them
by eye on every glance. Sessions now sit under a section per agent, and every
agent has a head: a neutral mark (an asterisk for Claude, a hexagon for Codex,
a diamond for anything else; no vendor logos), the agent's name, its plan
limits, and an alarm while something of its is blocked. The limits used to be
a strip of bars above the board; on a phone the heads still stand there.

How the sections are laid out depends on the room:

- **Where a 340pt column per agent fits** (two agents on any full-screen
  iPad, three on an iPad in landscape), each agent gets a column under its
  head, and the columns may run to different lengths. With more agents than
  that, the sections stack full width with cards two across, each head
  scrolling with its section under a pinned line of every agent's name and
  alarm. One agent alone gets a strip head across the top and its cards two
  across. The 340pt grows with the text size, so at larger sizes, and in
  Split View, an iPad steps down to the stack or the list.
- **On a phone** the sessions stay one list, under each agent's name. The
  heads stay pinned above it, two across, for up to two agents with up to
  four limit windows each. Four is the most either agent reports in ordinary
  use; one report may carry six, and six pinned two across took 63% of an
  iPhone SE's board. With three or more agents, or a fifth window in any
  head, the pinned line of names and alarms stands in for the heads, and each
  full head scrolls with its section, every window in it.
- **At an accessibility text size, or held sideways,** a phone uses that
  pinned line from the first agent, because two pinned heads with their
  limits would leave almost no room for the cards. Most iPhones are wide
  enough on their side for two columns, but a phone keeps the list there:
  only the list can be swiped, and 1.5.0 kept it in every orientation too.

A card keeps its place when its state changes, as before. When something is
blocked, the alarm in its agent's head (or in the pinned line) scrolls to the
first blocked card and rings it briefly. Every card on every device has a
long-press menu with Dismiss, and Bring to front when the session reports its
machine; the phone's list keeps its swipe as well. A session's title may take
two lines, and when it does the message takes one. A board whose agents report
limits but run no sessions says "Nothing running" instead of offering to pair
a computer.

The layout's rules, and the owner's decisions behind them, are in
[the prototype](https://github.com/KardanovIR/agstatus/blob/v1.6.5/docs/design/prototypes/agent-sections.html)
the board was built from.

**Audited before release.** The board was rendered on the simulator in every
arrangement and at large text sizes, and audited for accessibility, layout,
theming, performance and design. That audit found two things the new board
had made worse, both fixed here: the phone pinning two full heads at
accessibility text sizes (it left about 90pt for the cards on a 6.9" phone),
and the dots on a card's meta line drawn in a line colour that fell to about
1.5:1. Also fixed: a head with no limits is now a full 44pt control, its
VoiceOver hint says what it opens, and the streak bar no longer breaks words
at large text.

**Reviewed before release.** A review of the release itself found more, also
fixed here. Most iPhones held sideways took the iPad's columns, which cannot
be swiped and left about a card and a third under two pinned heads; they keep
the list now, as above. Where an agent's name and alarm do not fit on one
line of the pinned line, the alarm goes under the name instead of the name
being cut to a letter, as the head already did. The streak bar gives the day
count its words before the forecast, which is the part that drops out when
both do not fit. And a head with no limits keeps its name level with the head
beside it.

## Accessibility, and the rest, on iOS

The audit of the new board left three things for later, all of them in 1.5.0
already: a card wider than a phone at accessibility text sizes, a stale card's
dimmed text under WCAG AA, and reset times cut mid-number at the largest
sizes. A sweep of the board and the screens it opens found more, and an audit
after the sweep more again. All three are fixed here, and so is everything
below.

**A stale card keeps its words.** A card that is planning, coding or testing
and has heard nothing for ten minutes is most likely a dead agent or an
interrupted turn. It was drawn whole at 0.62 opacity, its message at 3.4:1 and
its meta line at 2.6:1, under AA on the card most worth a closer look, and
VoiceOver was told nothing. Now every word keeps its full strength, and the
card says what it is three other ways. It loses its tint and takes the plain
card's surface and edge, darker than any tinted card, where the meta line is
5.47:1 at the lowest. A "?" takes the place of the state's glyph, and the word
keeps its colour: "? CODING", what the board last heard, which it can no
longer vouch for. And "no update for 14m" stands where the time was, or "14m
ago" where those words do not fit. VoiceOver hears "Coding, no update for 14
minutes". Crossing the line is the board's own clock and not news, so it is
never animated, and the history a stale card opens shows the same "?".

**Cards at accessibility sizes.** From AX2 a card was wider than a phone, and
both its edges were cut off, taking the G of "CODING" and the first letter of
the name. A card
narrower than a board column at the reader's text size now stacks: its state
on a line of its own at the top, then its name and message, then Focus at the
trailing edge. There the name is never cut, and the message takes up to four
lines where it took one ("Approve: …"). Columns, and a list as wide as a
column, keep the state beside the text.

**What VoiceOver hears.** A card is one element: its agent and name, then its
state, which used to come after the whole meta line, then what it last said.
The machine, the folder, the time and the token count wait in the More
Content rotor, and times are said in words ("14 minutes", where "14m" could be
heard as a length). An alarm takes VoiceOver to the blocked card, and a name
in the pinned line takes it to that agent's head. Focus's spoken name starts
with the word on the button, and Focus and Resume say how they went as they
land ("Not running on MacBook. Resume is available").

With notifications off, VoiceOver now hears a session become blocked:
"Claude: data-pipeline needs you", or "2 sessions need you" when several block
within half a second. Only a block that arrives while the board is open is
news, so a board that has just connected says nothing of the blocks it finds,
and a session is not said again within a minute, the server's own wait before
it pushes the same session twice. With notifications on, their banner says it
instead.

The connection lamp in the toolbar is one element, "Board connection", with
its state as its value, where it was a bare "Reconnecting" that named nothing.
A drop is said once it has lasted five seconds ("Board connection lost.
Reconnecting."), and so is a reconnect that fails on coming back to the app
while the old cards are still on screen, since they read as live. Copying the
webhook URL is said too. Voice Control answers to a head, an alarm and a name
in the pinned line by short names ("Claude", "Claude usage", "Claude
blocked", "Blocked") as well as by their full sentences.

**The connection lamp.** It was one dot in four colours with a glow round it.
Live green and reconnecting teal are close, and with cards on screen a dropped
connection changed nothing else. It now tells its state by shape, as a panel
lamp does: a filled dot while live, a ring while connecting or reconnecting, a
broken ring when not connected, a cross for a board that is gone. It no
longer glows, it grows with the text to 15pt, and at the accessibility sizes a
long press shows it large with its word.

**Heads.** A reset countdown is whole, shorter or gone, never cut: "· resets
in 2h 17m", then "· 2h 17m", then nothing, where the largest standard text
size read "resets in 2h 1…", which is a different number. VoiceOver keeps the
words, said as words ("resets in 2 hours, 17 minutes"). A window's label is
offered the width before the countdown, and the percentage never gives way;
at an accessibility size the label takes two lines, where both of Claude's
weekly windows had read "WEEKLY…".

**Session history.** The relative times were 3.68:1 and are 5.88:1, and the
latest event's dot no longer glows. The header wore a pill, the state's word
on its colour, beside a filled chip; it is the card's own badge now, glyph and
word, with "?" on a stale session, beside the folder in an outlined box, and
VoiceOver hears it as one element ("Directory acme-api. Coding"). Each row's
state word starts with its glyph, so a row no longer tells its state by hue
alone. Messages take the lines they need at every text size, where three
lines cut the hook's 120-character prompts from xL on an iPhone SE, and at
large text the times go under the status instead of breaking mid-word
("Plan|ning"). Updates land in order, and a session that leaves the board
while its history is open keeps its name and its timeline on screen instead
of turning into "No history yet".

**The usage screen.** The limit lines differed by hue alone, and "week" and
"week_fable" drew as one blue line (0.044 apart in OKLab for a deuteranope).
Each line now has a pattern as well (solid, dashed, dotted, dash-dot, long
dash, dash-dot-dot) and is cased in the card's colour, so it holds where it
crosses a bar. The colours are reordered so that the first three, the most a
board usually has, are green, blue and lilac, with red, the alarm, last. A
legend key is a sample of its line, and the keys wrap as words do. A window
with a single reading, as every window has on the first day the board records
it, was a 5pt dot that differed from its neighbours by hue alone; it is drawn
as a shape of its own now, circle, plus, square, triangle, diamond and
triangle pointing down, paired with the colours by measuring them under
simulated colour-blind vision, and its key shows the shape. Readings within
about 4 points of each other on that day still overlap, as lines do.

The chart's text was a fixed 9pt on the axes and 11pt in the legend, under
Apple's floor and deaf to the reader's text size. It grows with it now, up to
AX2's 24pt on the axes, and the plot and its marks grow at half that rate. At
the accessibility sizes nothing on the screen is cut: the header's total takes
a line of its own, the title wraps at a word, and a project's name and a
legend key each take a row of their own where they do not fit. For VoiceOver
the chart was one element whose label mentioned the plan limit and gave none
of it. It is two now, the tokens and the plan limit, each with a summary and
an Audio Graph: the busiest day with its date, and for each window its latest
reading, its highest day and, where the board started recording it partway,
since when.

**Increase Contrast.** No colour read the setting. The quieter text and the
lines now take a second value under it: the faintest text goes from 4.63:1 to
6.60:1 at the least (on a blocked card, the narrowest margin on the board),
the secondary text from 6.60:1 to 8.70:1, every hairline (card and head
borders, the history's connectors, the usage chart's gridlines) from 1.43:1 to
3.23:1 on the ground, the card edges from 2.04–2.39:1 to 3.46–4.00:1 against
their own surfaces and blocked's from 1.93:1 to 3.05:1, and a limit meter's
empty track is brighter. At standard contrast nothing changes.

**Colours that failed AA.** The main buttons ("Pair your computer", "Start
over", and the main buttons of the welcome, pairing and scanner screens) drew
their white labels on the light accent green at 2.05:1, under even the 3:1
allowed for large text. They are a darker green of the same hue now, #0C7D48,
at 5.19:1. The swipe's white "Dismiss" on the blocked red goes from 3.15:1 to
5.13:1, the Bronze tier's name from 4.46:1 to 5.59:1, and "Pull down to
retry." takes the tertiary ink, 5.88:1, where it was below it.

**Smaller things.** The push tip's Enable (16pt tall) and its close (21pt) are
44pt targets now, and at an accessibility size its sentence comes first,
whole, where it was squeezed to "Ge…". The first-run webhook row is 44pt tall
and says "Copy" beside its glyph, a word Voice Control can be told. At an
accessibility size the streak bar's pips, which read as an ellipsis after the
tier's name, give way to the rank in words. The empty, connecting and "board
gone" screens fit a short phone and the accessibility sizes: an idle board's
heads scroll with the empty state wherever pinning them would cut names to a
letter or push "Nothing running" out of sight, as on a small phone held
sideways or at an accessibility size, and the board-gone page scrolls where
it does not fit. And the board animates a head gaining or losing a window,
not every usage report, so a card that changes in the same update as a
report, as on a reconnect, no longer cross-fades its old text into its new.

**Less work for each update.** On an iPad the cards stand in columns, new in
this release (1.5.0 drew every device as one list), and every hook event ran
and measured every card again: about 110 ms of main thread an event with 20
cards, and 330 ms with 50. An event now runs only its own card: about 25 ms
with 20 cards, and 32 ms with 50. The agents' heads and the pinned line also
ran on every event; they now run only when what they draw changes, which
takes a message event from 8.0–14.7 ms to 3.1–5.3 ms across the phone and
iPad boards measured. The once-a-minute tick that moves the countdowns costs
about 1.5 ms more than it did where a phone pins its heads. All of these were
measured in harnesses built from the app's source on a Mac with an M4 Pro,
not on a device.

**How it was checked.** On the iOS Simulator, on an iPhone SE, a 6.9" iPhone
and a 13" iPad, from the default text size to AX5, and held sideways.
VoiceOver does not run in the Simulator, so what it is told was written from
Apple's documentation and reviewed independently, not heard on a device.

## The web board

The web board takes iOS's chart, its contrast values and its stale cards, so
the clients draw the same things the same way again. The chart's lines and
more contrast have been live on agstatus.online since November 6; the rest is
new in this release.

**The usage chart's lines** (live since November 6). They differed by hue
alone, and in the old order "week" and "week_fable" were blue and teal and
drew as one blue line. They take iOS's order and patterns now, solid, dashed,
dotted, dash-dot, long dash and dash-dot-dot, each first stroked in the
card's colour 2.5 px wider, so that a line over a bar has the card beside it,
5.70:1 at the least, where uncased it was 1.83:1 to 2.81:1. A legend key is a
sample of its line, and the keys wrap as words do. The chart draws one px to
the px: it was a fixed viewBox scaled by CSS, which on a phone drew its axis
labels at 4.5 px and its lines 0.79 px wide. Its labels are the board's 11 px
tick type now and grow with the reader's text size, and the bars sit half a
bar in from the plot's edges, where the last ran half under "100%". A screen
reader hears a summary in iOS's words, the tokens and the busiest day, then
each window's latest reading, its highest day and since when, in place of an
image whose label named the plan limit and gave none of it; the two card
titles are headings.

**A window's single reading** (new). On the first day the board records a
window, the window has one reading, and the chart drew it as a 2.5 px dot that
differed from the other windows' by hue alone. Each window has a shape now as
well as a colour and a pattern, the same shapes on the same colours as on
iOS, and a single reading is drawn as that shape over a casing of its own
outline. Its key shows the shape while the window has one reading. A mark
grows with the plot at half the rate of the text, to 12 px at 200% text.
Readings within about 4 points of each other on that day still overlap, as
lines do.

**Each day's figures** (new). A day's figures were only in the bars'
tooltips, which no keyboard can hover and no phone shows, and a screen reader,
which hears the summary, got none of them. Under the chart, "Each day's
figures" now opens a table: a row a day, newest first, with a tokens column
and a column for each limit window, headed by the key the legend gives the
window, a sample of its line or, while it has one reading, its shape. Tokens
and percentages never share a column, and a percentage is the window's
reading as the day ended. It is a real disclosure, so Enter and Space open it,
and it stays open as the board updates. A table too wide for its card, such as
six windows on a phone, scrolls inside the card with its dates held, and the
page never widens.

**More contrast** (live since November 6). Nothing on the web read the
setting. Under `prefers-contrast: more`, which macOS's and iOS's "Increase
contrast" report to the page, the quieter inks and the hairlines take the
values iOS takes: the faintest text goes from 4.61:1 to 6.57:1 at its worst,
on a blocked card, the secondary text to 8.66:1, every hairline from 1.43:1 to
3.23:1 on the ground, the card edges from 2.04–2.36:1 to 3.45–4.00:1 and
blocked's from 1.92:1 to 3.06:1, and the limit meter's empty track is the top
ink at 14%. The landing, privacy and docs pages take the same values. The
status legend's zero counts, which the board dims, dim to 0.7 under the
setting instead of 0.45, which keeps them at 4.63:1. Standard contrast is
unchanged.

**Stale cards keep their words** (new). A stale card was the whole card at
0.62 opacity, its message at 3.42:1 and its meta line at 2.61:1, and a screen
reader was told nothing. It looks and reads as on iOS now: untinted, where the
meta line is the faintest text at 5.47:1 and the message 7.80:1; "? CODING",
the "?" in place of the state's mark and the word in its colour; and "no
update for 14m" where the time was, or "14m ago", still bright, where the
meta line is too narrow for the words. A screen reader hears "coding, no
update for 14 minutes" straight after the state. Crossing the line is never
animated, either way. Since November 6 a stale card under more contrast had
faded to 0.8 rather than 0.62; with the fade gone at every setting, that goes
too.

**A card's title says its state** (new). The title is a card's first control,
and a reader moving by Tab or by buttons heard only "api-server — open this
session's history" on every card: no state, and on a stale card nothing of
how long it had been quiet. The title is now described by the card's state
row and its message, the reading iOS gives a card, so a stale card's title
says "no update for 14 minutes" too. Its name stays the words on the button,
and the description is read from the live row, so the age stays current.

**Marks grow with the text** (new). Every mark that carries a state or labels
a control was sized in pixels while the words beside it grew: at 250% text a
state's mark was a third of its word's height. The state and stale marks, the
agent's mark, the Focus and Resume icons, the connection lamp and the
timeline's marks and dots now grow with the text, at the same size as before
at the default one.

**How it was checked.** In headless Chrome at 1280 and 375 px, at standard
and more contrast and at 200% and 300% text, each change against its own
branch's rendering and the merged board as a whole, which was also audited.
No screen reader was run; what one is told was read from the accessibility
tree.

The landing page's app section now says the iOS app shows the same sessions
grouped by agent, and its three phone images show the board by agent.

## Android

The Android app's stale cards match the other two: untinted, a "?" in the
state glyph's own box, and "no update for 14m" where the time was, or "14m
ago" where the words do not fit. TalkBack hears "Coding, no update for 14
minutes", and the history a stale card opens shows the same "?". A card's time
now sits on its meta line, where it rode about 4dp above it.

The usage chart takes the treatment iOS and the web have: the limit lines in
their order, done, coding, planning (it drew "week" and "week_fable" as one
blue line), each with its pattern and cased in the card's colour; a window's
single reading drawn as its shape; keys that are what the chart draws, and
wrap as words do. TalkBack hears the chart's figures in the other clients'
words, the tokens and the busiest day, then each window's latest reading, its
highest day and since when, where it heard one fixed sentence and none of the
numbers.

Debug builds gain demo switches for a stale card, a frozen demo, more limit
windows and first-day readings. It was checked on an emulator, where
TalkBack's text was read from the accessibility tree, not heard. The app is
still unpublished, at 1.6.2 (versionCode 6), so none of this reaches a phone
yet.

## Upgrading

**iOS.** Update from the App Store once 1.6.5 is through review. Building the
app yourself now takes Xcode 26 or later, for the iOS 26 SDK; it still runs on
iOS 17 and later.

**Everything else.** Nothing in the CLI, the hook or the plugin changed, and
the server's code is 1.6.4's. Re-running
`curl -fsSL https://agstatus.online/install.sh | sh` moves the installed
version to 1.6.5 and keeps the machine's setup, as it has since 1.6.4.
Updating the Claude Code plugin is optional; the version moves so the
installer, the plugin and the server image agree.

**Self-hosting.** The web board is served by the server, so a self-hosted
board gets this release's web changes, the two already on agstatus.online
among them, from the new image: pull `ghcr.io/kardanovir/agstatus:1.6.5`
(also tagged `1.6` and `latest`) and restart. With the repository's
`docker-compose.yml`, `git pull` and run `docker compose up -d --build` with
the `--profile` flags you always use. The file has not changed since 1.6.4,
so the one `down` that 1.6.4 asked for is not needed again. A server left on
1.6.4 serves the 1.6.5 iOS app as before, with 1.6.4's web board.

**Android.** Nothing to install: the app has not been published.
