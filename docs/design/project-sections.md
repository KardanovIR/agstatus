# AgStatus 1.7.0: the board grouped by project (implementation spec)

Ships together with Subtasks in 1.7.0, built on `claude/subtasks-1.7.0` (d0d8687). Subtasks' rows stay on their parent card, unchanged.

The build plan's step 0 (a prototype for sign-off) is skipped: the owner decided from the mockups below.

## Owner decisions (2026-11-08)

The owner asked: "Let's group tasks by the project the same way we group subagents", and confirmed it means the session cards on the AgStatus board. Asked to choose, the owner decided:

1. **Layout: project sections.** Each project gets a label that sticks while its cards scroll by, with its sessions as full cards under it (not project cards with session rows).
2. **Replace agent grouping on every client** (iPhone, iPad, web, Android). Agents stay as the pinned heads carrying their plan limits and session count, and as a mark on every card. No Agent | Project setting.
3. **The alarm is one needs-you bar at the bottom edge** on every client, present only while something is blocked. The agents' heads keep only their limits; the agent line retires.
4. **Ship together with Subtasks as 1.7.0**, built on the subtasks branch.

Defaults taken from the design's recommendations (not separately asked): a project is the folder a session started in, pinned by the server (`homeProject`); wide boards stack full-width project sections with cards two across under full-width head strips; the web keeps each card's place (new cards on top of their section, nothing re-sorts on a tool call).

## Owner decisions (2026-11-16)

The owner asked whether there was another way to show groups. Two rounds of exploration drew alternatives: needs-you first, the project on the card, project lamps, sections packed on wide boards, session rows under the labels, and a bar listing every blocked session. The owner chose:

- **1.7.0 ships the project sections as built.**
- **Session rows come to the web after 1.7.0, as a deploy.** Each session is a two-line row under the same labels, and only a blocked row is boxed. Whether the apps follow is decided after the owner has lived with it.
- **A list of every blocked session in the bar is not planned for now.**

Later that day the owner took the recommended answer to each of three gaps the final verification raised: two in the needs-you bar, from its design audit, and a screen-reader leftover from 1.6.5, from its completeness critic.

5. **The second block: the bar replays its arrival, once per rise in the count.** While the bar shows, a session that blocks out of view, or behind the walk, changed one glyph on the screen, the bar's count, 4 to 5: 41 px on the web at 1440 × 900, and about 0.01% of the screen on iOS and Android. On a second monitor nobody notices it, against CLAUDE.md's "a change has to be noticeable without being looked at directly" and its principles 2 and 3. A tick on the digit would not be enough either: it moves a 0.2-degree glyph about 5 px. So when the blocked count rises while the bar already shows, the bar comes in from the bottom edge again, once, with the new count already in it, by the motion and timing of its own first arrival: on the web, needs-in (--d-slow, --ease) restarted on an inner layer of the bar that carries its ground, edge and words, while the button's own box stays put, so a click never lands on a card behind a bar in motion; on iOS, the same slide with the bar's .snappy, as an .offset on NeedsYouBar; on Android, the bar's 220 ms BarEase, as a graphicsLayer translation of the bar's column. It is drawn only: the bar is never hidden and shown, and iOS's transition and Android's AnimatedVisibility, which change the board's height, never run again. The transitions the voice already detects drive it, gathered over the same 500 ms, so blocks that land together make one beat, and at most once a minute per session. Only reduced motion, the bar not on screen, and the app not in front or the tab hidden keep it still; the voice's other silences, push on and VoiceOver off, do not. Under reduced motion nothing moves: the count changes in place and the voice carries the news. It must not:
   - pulse: one beat per rise in the count, never a loop, a reminder while blocks stand unanswered, a beat on a fall (an unblock or a dismiss), or a beat when one block replaces another at the same count;
   - glow: no shadow, blur, bloom, brighter ground, colour change, ring or flash on the bar;
   - move cards: the bar's layout box, the web's page padding (--needs-h) and scroll padding, iOS's board frame, Android's Scaffold insets and list padding, the scroll position and focus all stay put;
   - repeat for blocks that were already there: never on first paint, an SSE snapshot or a reconnect, a refetch, or a return from another screen or the background, never for a session already said within its minute, never for the first block, whose arrival is already the beat, and never while the bar is entering or leaving;
   - speak or retarget: no new words on the bar beyond the count, no focus move and no extra announcement, since the voice already says it once;
   - move under reduced motion.
6. **One ground for the bar on every client: the blocked card's surface and edge.** iOS drew the bar on the blocked card's surface under the blocked card's edge. The web and Android drew it on a card's neutral ground, 1.07:1 against the page, under the neutral hairline, so at rest it could hardly be found without reading it, against decision 3's one bar on every client. The web takes the blocked card's own expression, `color-mix(in oklab, var(--ink-900) 86%, var(--st-blocked))`, under the blocked card's edge (card.css's `--state-line` for blocked, which `prefers-contrast: more` raises), using the tokens and mixes card.css uses rather than new literals, with a hover derived from the same mix and never a brighter glow. Android takes `Theme.cardSurface(BLOCKED)`, with the blocked card's edge as its divider. iOS is unchanged. Every ink on the bar must still clear WCAG AA on the new ground, measured one by one: the red mark, count and word, the primary session name, and the secondary place and verb, under increased contrast too. The web's focus ring inside the bar must still clear 3:1 against it.
7. **The NVDA "dot", on the web (a 1.6.5 leftover).** A card title button is described by its state row (aria-describedby), and Chrome joins the row's boxes with spaces, so the description read 'CODING , no update for 14 minutes .' and NVDA, at its default punctuation, said "dot". One visually hidden element holds the row's id and the whole sentence inline, as in 'Coding, no update for 14 minutes.', its stale words an inline span inside it; the visible badge and marks are hidden from the tree, and the layout measures the same as before. The card's repaint and the stale timer keep the sentence current, as they keep the visible row. In Chrome's computed accessibility tree the description has no space before a comma or a full stop, for a fresh card, a stale card, a card with a message, and after a state change.

## Wire contract (server ↔ clients)

- Every session object on REST (`/api/sessions`, `/w/:token/api/sessions`) and SSE (snapshot and upserts) gains `homeProject: string`.
- The server derives it and never accepts it from a post: the session's previous `homeProject` if non-empty; else the session's current `project` if that is a **usable folder name** (below), and `''` if not. Once non-empty it never changes for the session's life (a later `cd` never moves a card).
- A **usable folder name**: a string can name a folder (a section, or the place a card says it is) only when, with the white space at its ends trimmed, it is non-empty, it is not `/` and not `.`, and it is not UUID-shaped (the hook's `UUID_NAME_RE`). Every test is on the trimmed value; the name itself is used as the server serves it, in NFC (below), and never trimmed. The server's normalisation returns `''` for anything else. The legacy shell hook posts `/` for a session started at `/`, because POSIX `basename /` is `/` (the Node hook posts `''`); white space alone and a padded UUID come from raw posts, or from a folder so named. Each platform trims with its own white-space set, and the sets differ only at U+0085 (Swift trims it, JavaScript and Kotlin do not) and U+FEFF (JavaScript trims it, Swift and Kotlin do not). No hook sends either, and the server already strips U+0000–U+001F and U+007F, so only a raw post built for it can open a section on one client that another files under No project; that is accepted.
- **Unicode form.** The server stores and serves a session's `project` and `name` in Unicode NFC. It composes them on the way in, on every route that accepts a session (the legacy and `/w/:token` webhooks), before it cuts them to 120 UTF-16 units, and its `normalizeProject` returns NFC, so a home worked out at load from an old row is NFC too; an old row's own name and project are composed as it loads. One folder written in two forms on two machines (a folder made in the macOS Finder is often named decomposed, NFD; a Linux checkout of it composed, NFC) is then one section on every client. Before it composes a posted value, the server cuts it to 512 UTF-16 units, without splitting a pair. Composing puts each run of combining marks in canonical order, at a cost that grows with the square of the run: composed whole, one 16 KB post of marks in the worst order held the server's one thread for about 60 ms, and one free board spending its minute's 120 posts at once held it for about 8 s, long enough for other boards' hooks to give up. 512 units lose nothing a name needs. A composed character stands for at most four UTF-16 units (polytonic Greek: an alpha with psili, oxia and ypogegrammeni), so the 120 units kept never come from more than the first 480, and nothing a hook sends reaches 512: a folder name is at most 255 bytes, and the Node hook cuts a title to 120 units. Only a raw post with a run of more than about 30 combining marks across unit 480 can come out other than composing all of it would leave it, and in NFC all the same; that is accepted.
- **A blank name is no name.** The server treats a session name of white space alone as no name, as it already treats an empty one: it keeps the session's previous name, or names the card by its id. White space alone is judged on the name as the server keeps it, its control characters stripped, cut to 512 UTF-16 units, composed, and cut to 120: a name that then trims to nothing as JavaScript trims, with U+0085 counted too, as Swift counts it, so a name that any client would trim to nothing is caught. Nothing else is: a name made only of characters that draw nothing but are not white space, such as U+200B (ZERO WIDTH SPACE) or U+3164 (HANGUL FILLER), is kept as sent, so its card can show no name; that is a known residual. The server applies this on every route that accepts a session, and to a row an older server stored so, as it loads (in memory only; the session's next write stores the name), so no client is sent a name of white space alone. Only a raw post, or a hook for an untitled session in a folder named with white space alone, sends one. A server older than 1.7.0 passes one on as it is; iOS and the web then read the card as its agent and a comma, and Android as its agent and the session's id. Clients need no change for this, and an older server's blank name is accepted.
- A card the board drops on request (dismissed, which is also what the hook's `SessionEnd` sends; cleared; or evicted by the 50-card cap) keeps its `homeProject` for its session's return: a post within 24 hours of the session's last one files the new card under it. A card the TTL expires keeps nothing, because the hook's pinned name ages out with it and the two start over together. The server holds this in memory only, in a bounded map per board, so a restart forgets it and such a session starts over from the folder it then reports.
- Persisted in a new column `home_project TEXT` (ALTER TABLE … ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS; on load a missing value is derived from the row's project, and derived again at every load until the session's next write stores it, so an older server writing the row in between can change it: see Risks). Nothing is deleted.
- Clients compute the **section key**: `homeProject` if it is a usable folder name; else `project` if it is one (a server too old to send the field); else the **No project** bucket.
- **Comparing names.** Clients compare folder names, section keys and names by UTF-16 code unit, which is exact. For NFC text this agrees with Swift's canonical equality, but iOS must not rely on that: an older server sends whatever it got. This covers the section key, a moved card's 'in <folder>', the bar's place elision and a card named after its agent. One comparison on iOS still uses Swift's own equality: a session's synthesized Equatable, so a post that changed only the Unicode form of a name or folder would not draw its card again. That cannot happen in practice: every post also moves `updatedAt`, unless two land within the same millisecond.
- **Section order**, identical on every client: compare the names lowercased, by UTF-16 code unit, then the exact names, by UTF-16 code unit (`AgStatus` before `agstatus`), never by a locale's collation; **No project** always last. Never by activity, state or arrival. Lowercasing applies Unicode's final-sigma rule, as JavaScript's `toLowerCase` and Kotlin's `lowercase(Locale.ROOT)` do: a capital sigma becomes `ς` (U+03C2), not `σ`, when a cased letter comes before it and none after it, looking past case-ignorable characters only, such as an apostrophe, a full stop, a colon or a combining mark (a digit, a hyphen, an underscore or a space ends the look). So the `Σ` of `ΝΟΜΟΣ` and of `ΑΣ1` becomes `ς`, and a `Σ` alone, or the one in `ΑΣ.Z`, becomes `σ`. iOS uses `lowercased(with: nil)`, and its tests check that it applies the rule (Swift's `lowercased()` does not). At the rule's corners, such as a `Σ` before punctuation and another letter, the platforms do not all agree; Risks records where.
- **Shared test vector** (every client's tests assert this exact order): input `["zeta", "", "Alpha", "alpha", "beta", "AgStatus", "agstatus", "0f3c2a1e-6b4d-4e8f-9a7b-1c2d3e4f5a6b"]` → sections `["AgStatus", "agstatus", "Alpha", "alpha", "beta", "zeta", <No project>]` (the empty name and the UUID both fall into No project). Every client's order tests also assert two pairs:
  - the final-sigma pair `ΝΟΜΟΣ` and `νομος-api`, which order `[ΝΟΜΟΣ, νομος-api]` (without the rule, `ΝΟΜΟΣ` lowercases to `νομοσ`, which sorts after);
  - `café` composed and decomposed, given as raw values from a server older than NFC: two sections, by code unit, the decomposed one first (`e`, U+0065, sorts before `é`, U+00E9).
- **Card order inside a section**: the board's own stable order, filtered and never re-sorted: a new session goes on top of its section, every other card keeps its place.
- **The usage history's order** (decision 10). The server sorts what the usage screens read by UTF-16 code unit, as the clients compare names, and never by `localeCompare` or a locale's collation (`byCodeUnit`, src/store.ts): the usage list by source; the history's limit series by source, then window id; its project days by day, then source, then project (docs/api.md). A collation composes and reorders a name's combining marks on every comparison, at a cost that grows with the square of a run of them; it also follows the server's locale and calls some different names equal. Measured on Node 20.9, by the fix and again by its exercise, with one free board holding 5,000 project days named with 120 units of combining marks in the worst order: one usage-history read took 1.1 to 1.6 s at 0a6fd33 and 7 to 16 ms after the fix; 30 reads at once took 33 to 45 s to be answered, and 0.19 to 0.29 s after; another board's hook posts sent meanwhile waited 1.3 s or timed out at 3 s, and were answered within 0.21 s after; the sort alone took 1.2 to 2.8 s, and 2.4 to 4.8 ms after. The rows are the same, and only their order moves, where names differ by case, by '-', '_' or a digit at the same place, outside ASCII, or in Unicode form (`Zeta` before `alpha`, `claude-x` before `claude2` before `claude_x`). Every client ranks an agent's folders on the usage screen by spend, so there this order only breaks ties between folders that spent the same.

## The recommended design (synthesis of three designs and two judges)

Group the board by project on every client, the way it groups by agent today: a section per project, a quiet label that sticks while its cards scroll by, sections in a fixed order, and cards that never move except to arrive or leave.

A project is the folder a session started in. The server will remember it for the life of the session. That takes one new column in the production database and nothing reinstalled on your machines. So an agent that runs cd ios keeps its place, and its card just adds 'in ios'. A Claude worktree counts as its repository, as it does today (with the Node hook; see WHAT A PROJECT IS). Sessions with no usable folder share a 'No project' section at the end. Sections run in name order and never reorder because one got busy.

Two things can't belong to a project, so they stay with the agents:
- Plan limits belong to an agent's account. Each agent keeps its head, with every limit and its session count. On a phone the heads are pinned two across, by the same rules as 1.6.5. On an iPad they become full-width strips.
- Claude and Codex now share sections, so every card's meta line starts with its agent's neutral mark again.

The alarm becomes one bar. There are too many projects to keep every label in view, so an alarm per section can't work the way the alarms in the agent heads did. Instead, while anything is blocked, a bar along the bottom edge names the session and its project: '2 BLOCKED · Fix the login redirect · in acme-api · Show'. Press it and that card comes into view with a two-second ring; press again for the next one. It's the same bar on iPhone, iPad, web and Android, so the web and Android get their first alarm. The heads lose their alarms, so red in a head can only mean a limit.

Smaller changes:
- A label carries only a name and a count: no colour, no tap.
- 'in <folder>' appears only when a session has moved away from its project's folder.
- On the iPad, the agent columns give way to full-width sections with cards two across.
- The web stops moving the latest card to the top on every tool call.

Nothing new is coloured, nothing moves unless something changed, and 1.6.5's rules for large text, a phone on its side and VoiceOver carry over.

Five decisions are yours (below). The two that matter most are retiring the agent sections and moving the alarm. Everything is drawn first in a prototype for you to sign off before any code is written.

## Behaviour rules

- WHAT A PROJECT IS. It is the folder a session started in. The server pins the first usable folder name it hears for each session and keeps it for the session's life. With the Node hook a Claude worktree root already reports its repository; the deprecated bash hook posts the worktree's own folder, so there each worktree is a section of its own. A later cd never moves the card, and nor does a dismissal: a card dismissed, cleared or evicted comes back to its section when its session posts again within a day of its last post, unless the server restarted in between. Some sessions have no usable folder: an agent started at '/', a Codex scratch folder, a folder named with white space alone, or a raw webhook post with a blank folder or none. They sit in 'No project' and move once, when a real folder arrives. The server keeps a folder's name in Unicode NFC, so a name that reaches it in two forms is one project. On a server too old to send the field, clients fall back to the live folder.
- SECTION ORDER. Sections sort by name: the names lowercased, with Unicode's final-sigma rule, and compared by UTF-16 code unit, then the exact names the same way (AgStatus before agstatus). The rule is computed identically on iOS, the web and Android. 'No project' is always last. Sections never reorder by activity, state or arrival time. A new project takes its alphabetical place, and the sections after it move down as its first card arrives.
- CARD ORDER INSIDE A SECTION. Cards follow the board's own order, filtered and never re-sorted. A new session goes on top of its section, then every card keeps its place, blocked included. A failed dismiss puts a card back where it was. Where a frame of the stream brought the card back while its dismiss was still on its way to the server, the web puts it back where it was too, while iOS and Android put it on top of its section, where it stays if the dismiss is then refused (Calls still open for the owner). The web adopts this rule; today it re-sorts by last update on every event.
- THE LABEL. Every section has one, including a one-card project and the only project on the board. It shows the folder name in exact case (semibold) and 'N sessions' (tertiary ink, tabular digits) on one line. When space runs short, the count goes before any letter of the name, then the name is cut in the middle. 'No project' is set in secondary ink at regular weight. A label is not a control and carries no mark, colour, limit, token total or alarm.
- STICKING. A label sticks on its own opaque ground while its cards scroll under it: on the phone in every mode, text size and orientation, on Android, and on the web at every width. On the iPad it scrolls with its section. The iPad's stacks are eager so that a jump lands exactly, and eager stacks cannot pin headers. At the end of the list a stuck label can rest with none of its cards showing, after a press near the end or, on Android, a plain scroll to the end: that is List and LazyColumn mechanics, on iOS and Android alike, and accepted.
- SPACING DOES THE GROUPING, as in 1.6.5: 24 pt between sections, 12 between cards, 8 from a label to its first card. Nothing is boxed.
- THE AGENTS' HEADS. Every agent with sessions on the board has a head on every client, whether or not it reports plan limits; on an empty board, every agent that reports limits has one, as before. A report with no windows is no report. The heads come in one order on every client, the empty board included: by the agent's key, compared by UTF-16 code unit (claude, claude-work, codex, gemini); a key is a-z, 0-9, '_' and '-' only, where Swift's own string order, which iOS sorts by, agrees. Never by activity, and never by the order the usage arrives in. A head shows the mark, the name, the session count and every plan limit. The count is all of the agent's cards on the board, done included ('7 sessions', '1 session'), and it is always in the head's accessible name. It is drawn beside the name where it fits, and it is the first thing to go when room runs short, dropped whole before any letter of the name. On iOS that holds in every form a head takes, so at larger text the heads that lead the list keep the count where it fits; at 0a6fd33 they lost it, and 'No plan limits reported' with it, on an SE from extra large text, on a 6.9-inch phone from extra extra extra large, and at every accessibility size, though their line had room (decision 9). An iOS head that shares its row never draws it: two heads pinned side by side above the one-list board, on a phone, as Frame A draws them, or on an iPad where two columns do not fit, where at 0a6fd33 two heads wide enough for the regular form drew it (Calls still open for the owner). On the web and Android each agent's usage block is its head: it shows the count beside the agent's name where it fits, in the label count's quiet ink. A head whose agent reports no limits shows its mark, its name and its count like any other, and in place of meters one sentence, 'No plan limits reported', in the count's quiet ink: in every usage block on the web and Android, and on every iOS head but those pinned above the one-list board (a phone, or an iPad where two columns do not fit), which show their one line alone, since there a sentence about what is absent would cost a line of every screen. On iOS the sentence, too, is drawn in every form a head takes, and on every client it takes a second line where one is too short, rather than losing a word (two lines on an SE at AX5). It is the same button as any head, '<agent>, <N> sessions'. A tap opens that agent's 30 days of usage. Until the agent reports any usage at all, that screen reads 'limit history starts once reported' and 'No per-project token data reported yet.', its chart's spoken summary ends 'none reported', and its token total reads '0 total' over a token axis that tops out at 1, on every client (Calls still open for the owner). Heads lose the alarm and the blocked edge.
- WHERE THE HEADS GO. Phone, unchanged from 1.6.5: pinned two across while there are at most two agents with at most four windows each, at a standard text size, upright. Otherwise they become the first rows of the list and scroll away. Wide boards: full-width strips, one per row. A lone agent's strip is always pinned. Two are pinned at standard text sizes with at most four windows each. From a third agent, the strips lead the scroll. On Android the usage blocks are pinned while the list keeps room under them for a label, the shortest card and the needs-you bar, and otherwise lead the list as its first item (ANDROID). The agent line retires.
- AGENTS: NAMES AND MARKS. An agent's display name is 'Claude' for claude and 'Codex' for codex. Any other source keeps its spelling with its first letter upper-cased, which is the hook's agentLabel rule: gemini is 'Gemini', codex-cli 'Codex-cli', claude-work 'Claude-work'. The web looks a name up with Object.hasOwn, never a bare object index, because SOURCE_NAMES['constructor'] is a function. The mark goes by the source's start: one starting with 'claude' gets the asterisk, one starting with 'codex' the hexagon, anything else the diamond.
- CARDS. The agent's neutral mark leads the meta line on every card, always. It never drops, and it is hidden from VoiceOver, which hears the agent in the card's label. 'in <folder>' shows only when the live folder is a usable folder name and differs from both the section and the name, compared by UTF-16 code unit, that is, when the session has moved. On iOS the other facts give way in one order on every card: machine and place, then machine, then place, then nothing, and none of them is ever cut to an ellipsis. The web and Android never drop them: they wrap the meta line onto more lines, and cut a fact wider than a line, a long machine or folder name, at its end with an ellipsis; that is accepted. The rest of the card is unchanged by the sections: the name, the state, the message, the rail, Focus, the long-press menu with Dismiss, the swipe, the stale words and the tokens. The name takes up to two lines on iOS (every line it needs at the accessibility sizes), and on an Android card that stacks its state above the name, as one does at a larger text size or in a narrow window; beside the state, Android gives it one line, cut at its end. The web gives it one line at every width, cut at its end, as 1.6.5 did; that is accepted until the session rows planned for the web after 1.7.0 replace that layout.
- THE NEEDS-YOU BAR: WHERE AND WHAT. It is present only while at least one session is blocked; done is never counted. On iOS it sits between the board and the streak. On the web it is fixed to the bottom edge, above the safe-area inset, and the page is padded so nothing hides under it. On Android it is the screen's bottom bar. It reads '(!) 2 BLOCKED · <session> · in <project> · Show'. The mark, count and word are in the blocked ink, the session in primary ink, the project and the verb in secondary ink. Its ground and its top edge are a blocked card's own, its surface and its edge, on every client (decision 6): iOS draws `Theme.cardSurface(for: .blocked)` under `Theme.cardEdge(for: .blocked)`, as it always has, Android `Theme.cardSurface(BLOCKED)` under `Theme.cardEdge(BLOCKED)`, and the web `color-mix(in oklab, var(--ink-900) 86%, var(--st-blocked))` under a 1 px edge of the blocked ink at 42%, or 70% under `prefers-contrast: more`, as card.css writes them for a blocked card. So at rest the bar is a band the corner of the eye can find, not a line of text: its ground is 1.27:1 against the page (1.28:1 on the web), where a card's neutral ground is 1.07:1, and its edge 2.45:1 (2.46:1), or under increased contrast 3.88:1 on iOS and 3.90:1 on the web. Android does not read the system's contrast setting, there or anywhere else in the app, so its edge stays at 2.45:1. Every ink on the bar clears AA, at either contrast: the mark, count and word 4.82:1 (4.80:1 on the web), the session 12.75:1 (12.70:1), the place and the verb 6.60:1 (6.57:1). The dot before the place is in the place's ink on iOS and Android, and in the tertiary ink on the web, 4.61:1. A keyboard's focus on the bar is a ring inside it, on the web and on Android (decision 8): a square line 2 px wide on the web and 2 dp on Android, in the accent, the web's `--st-done` and Android's `Theme.accent`, both oklch(76% 0.125 155), #69C88E. Its outer edge stands 2 px (2 dp) inside the bar's edges, so 1 px (1 dp) of ground shows between the blocked edge along the top and the ring. On the web it is the page's focus outline set inside the button (`outline-offset: -4px`), 7.38:1 against the bar's ground. On Android its outer edge also stands 2 dp inside the system bars and any cutout beside or under the bar (ANDROID), and it is 7.41:1 against the bar's ground and 9.41:1 against the page, which shows at the bar's place in a beat. Under focus the ground and every ink keep their resting values. On Android at 0a6fd33, Material's focus layer, drawn over the bar, lightened the ground to #423732 and took the red word to 3.95:1, under AA, the session to 9.67:1, and the place and verb to 5.37:1. Under the pointer on the web, and under a finger or a pointer on Android, which draws neither a ripple nor a focus layer there, the ground holds and the verb lights, from the secondary ink to the primary. A lighter ground would spend the blocked word's margin: the blocked mix over the next surface step, as a usage block's hover lifts its own, takes the 12 px blocked word to 4.27:1 and the web's dot to 4.10:1, and Material's press, hover and focus layers, drawn over the words, take the red word under AA as well. iOS dims the words under a finger, as it did. Whether that is the hover decision 6 asked for is the owner's call (Calls still open for the owner). It is one line on a wide screen: on the web wider than 560 px, on Android with 600 dp for it, and on iOS on a board two columns wide and on a phone held sideways. Narrower, as on a phone upright, it takes two lines, the alarm over the session. On one line it gives way in one order: the word BLOCKED (the mark and count stay), then the place, then the name is cut at its end. So on one line the word never stays once the place has gone, even where it would fit beside the name alone. That order was settled in review, and it stands unless the owner changes it. On an iOS board two columns wide the bar takes its second line instead of cutting the name; the web, Android and a phone held sideways cut it. On two lines each line gives way on its own: the session line drops the place, then cuts the name at its end, and the alarm line drops the word BLOCKED only when that line itself lacks room, since dropping it there frees nothing on the session line. The session is the card's name, or its agent's display name when it has none. The place is 'in <section>' for a folder section, left out when the section equals the name; for 'No project' it is the agent's display name instead, left out when that equals the name. Both comparisons are exact, by UTF-16 code unit. The whole bar is one button, at least 44 pt tall.
- PRESSING THE BAR. It brings the card it names into view: under its stuck label in a list, or 8 pt below what is pinned on a stack. The card gets a neutral ring for 2 s (gone sooner if the card is unblocked), and VoiceOver, TalkBack or keyboard focus moves onto it. - THE BAR'S BEAT. The bar's arrival, replayed once for a rise in the blocked count while it shows (decision 5). The rule is the same on every client, and the web's and Android's have been checked case for case against iOS's.
  - What moves. On the web the bar is a button holding a face that draws its ground, edge and words (WEB). SwiftUI draws the bar's ground under the home indicator (34 pt on a phone, 20 pt on an iPad) only while the bar, as drawn, meets the safe area's edge to within half a device pixel. It gathers them over the voice's half second, which runs from the first block and is not drawn out by later ones, so blocks that land together make one beat.
  - The count the bar rests on is a count that stood for half a second, or that a half second ended on, and none while the bar is empty. A rise is measured from the count the bar rested on when the half second's first block landed (Calls still open for the owner).
  - When the half second ends, and never while the bar is arriving or leaving, the bar beats only if all of these hold:
    - one of its blocks is still blocked;
    - the bar rested on a count when the first block landed, and has not emptied since: so never for the first block, whose arrival is the beat, nor for a block that lands before the bar has rested on any count, as a block gathered in the half second that starts with the bar's arrival does;
    - the count is higher than the one it rested on: so never on a fall, nor for one block in place of another within half a second of it, whichever lands first, where the bar was resting on the count before them both when the first landed and no half second ends between them (Calls still open for the owner);
    - the board was in front of the reader when the first block landed, and is when the half second ends, each read as it is then, and not in between: so never for a block that landed while the reader was away, nor on the way back, nor when the reader leaves within the half second;
    - motion is not reduced, as the setting stands when the half second ends.
  - In front. On the web: the tab showing, with no history or usage screen over the board. So a card partly under the label, as the first card in view usually is after a scroll, comes down to just under it, one cut off at the list's foot comes up, and a list stopped part-way into usage blocks that lead it goes back to its top for the first block. Where the usage blocks lead the list, a block that takes a keyboard's focus is brought into view below the blocks' own top padding, not under a label, since no label sticks above the list's first item. So Shift+Tab or the Up arrow from a card back into blocks the list had scrolled past puts the list back at its top, as a fresh board has it, and with animations off the pull-to-refresh is never left pulled. TalkBack's own focus is not handed on: when the bar leaves under it, TalkBack loses its place, and the next swipe starts again at the top of the screen. Compose tells the app nothing when TalkBack's focus moves, and with animations off the bar is gone before the board could ask where that focus was; that is accepted. A block is spoken only on a session's transition into blocked: never on the first draw, after a reconnect or for a card a refused dismiss puts back, and at most once a minute for each session. Only the stream's frames bring a transition (in the apps' demo, its ticks): a card the board puts back itself, after a dismiss the server refused, made none, and nor did a block that a fetch, such as a pull to refresh, brings in ahead of the stream. Neither is spoken, on any client, and nor is a frame that then only repeats it. Once such a card is answered, its next block is spoken like any other. On iOS such a block is standing, one the store took in with no frame of the stream: a card put back, or one a fetch or a snapshot brought (StandingBlocks). A frame that arrives while a dismiss is still on its way to the server brings the card back. If the card is blocked and still on the board when the half second it is gathered in closes (below), because its DELETE is slower than that or is refused, every client speaks it as a new block unless it was spoken within the minute, though the session never left blocked on the server. Where that frame landed half a second or more after the dismiss took the card away, the count has also risen past the one the bar rested on, and the bar beats for it by its own rule (THE BAR'S BEAT; Calls still open for the owner). A DELETE that lands first takes the card away again before anything is said or the bar beats: the server sends its remove as the DELETE lands, and that reaches the board less than a round trip after the frame did. Blocks that land within 500 ms of the first are said together (the half second runs from the first block, and later ones do not draw it out), as '<N> sessions need you'; a single block is '<name>, <place>, needs you', as in 'Fix the login redirect, in acme-api, needs you'. The commas set the place off, so without a place it is a plain sentence, '<name> needs you', as in 'Codex needs you', which is how iOS has said it. iOS also keeps today's silences: nothing when push is on, VoiceOver is off, or the app is not active when the half second ends, read as it is then (its scene's phase and UIKit's own state), so a block is not said when the reader leaves the app within half a second of it. The web says the same through a polite live region, never on page load. Android says it on every screen while the app is in front (resumed): the alarm's voice sits above the NavHost, not in the board screen, so a block that lands while History, Usage or Settings is open is said there, and is not lost while counted as said. While the app is not in front nothing is said, and what arrives then is dropped, not kept for later. When a block lands while TalkBack's focus rests on the bar and TalkBack is quiet, it first reads the bar's new name, which can still name the card it named before, and only then, about 5 s later in the exercise, the spoken sentence. If TalkBack is still speaking when the block lands, the sentence comes next and the re-read is dropped: of the changes queued behind what it is saying, TalkBack keeps only the latest. Holding the bar's name back until the sentence was out was tried and dropped: a busy TalkBack then never said the sentence. The order is accepted: either way the sentence is said. A beat of the bar (THE BAR'S BEAT) adds nothing here, on any client: no announcement, and no change to the bar's name, hint or Voice Control names beyond what the count already changed. iOS keeps the bar's accessibility frame at its place while what the bar draws moves. On Android the bar's node, its words and bounds, and what TalkBack speaks are as they are without a beat, and TalkBack's focus box holds the bar's place. On the web the button, which carries the bar's name, keeps its box. TalkBack's re-read of the bar's new name before the news, above, is as it was.
- HONEST NUMBERS. Tokens stay on each card. No label or head sums them, because Claude's and Codex's counts are not comparable. Limits never sit on a label and are never divided among projects.
- A PROJECT IS A FOLDER NAME ONLY, as the privacy promise requires. The same name on two machines is one section, whichever Unicode form each machine wrote it in. One repository checked out under two names is two sections. Two repositories that share a name are one.
- UNCHANGED: the idle board (every reporting agent's head and 'Nothing running', with no labels and no bar), the connecting state, the streak, the push tip, stale cards, the usage screen and its per-folder spend, but for the order of folders that spent the same (Wire contract: the usage history's order), and Subtasks' rows, which stay on their parent card.
